Lesson overview

Learning objectives

By the end of the lesson, you should be able to discuss the relevance of qualitative research in different fields and explain how that relevance varies between fields.

Fields discussed: Social sciences, media communication, business, and public health.

Opening review: Kinds of Qualitative Research

These six answers are identified from the definitions in the questions. The slides show the choices but do not provide an answer key.

Question 1
This qualitative approach focuses on exploring the essence of lived experiences of individuals.
A.Case Study
B.Phenomenology
C.Ethnography
D.Grounded Theory
Show answer and explanation

B. Phenomenology

Phenomenology examines the meaning of lived experiences.

Question 2
This research design aims to describe and interpret cultural patterns, beliefs, and behaviors of a group.
A.Ethnography
B.Historical Review
C.Narrative Inquiry
D.Grounded Theory
Show answer and explanation

A. Ethnography

Ethnography studies a group’s culture and shared practices.

Question 3
This design involves an in-depth examination of a single individual, group, event, or setting within a real-life context.
A.Phenomenology
B.Case Study
C.Narrative Inquiry
D.Historical Review
Show answer and explanation

B. Case Study

A case study investigates a particular case in depth.

Question 4
This qualitative approach seeks to generate or build a theory from patterns that emerge in collected data.
A.Grounded Theory
B.Phenomenology
C.Ethnography
D.Case Study
Show answer and explanation

A. Grounded Theory

Grounded theory develops theory from patterns in collected data.

A.Grounded Theory
B.Case Study
C.Phenomenology
D.Narrative Inquiry

D. Narrative Inquiry

Narrative inquiry focuses on people’s stories.

Slides 14–16

Qualitative Research in Social Sciences

Human learning

Qualitative research equips academics, professionals, and ordinary people with knowledge about society that can help improve their lives.

It helps people understand changes in society by paying attention to human experiences and context.

Slides 17–20

Qualitative Research in Media Communication

Media needs research

Qualitative research helps generate news content.

Researchers and journalists gather information, build rapport with interviewees, and ask appropriate interview questions. It also helps raise public social consciousness.

Slides 21–23

Qualitative Research in Business

Business analysis

Companies producing goods and offering services invest in research.

Qualitative research provides knowledge for sound business strategies, a suitable commercial image, and improved profitability.

Slides 24–26

Qualitative Research in Public Health

Communicable and noncommunicable diseases

Qualitative research supports the documentation and analysis of public health issues.

It can be used in epidemiological studies to examine how human behavior and practices contribute to disease transmission and mortality rates.

Slides 27–28, 33–36

Remember

Qualitative research is relevant in sociology, history, medicine, media communication, business, and other sciences.

It creates knowledge grounded in facts and helps people better understand themselves and society.

Main idea: Its relevance changes by field: understanding social change, producing informed media, shaping business strategies, or documenting public health issues.

Apply your learning

Think of a recurring issue within your strand. Identify why qualitative research would be important for studying that issue.
